Nice Nubra in Ladakh

By Bhupender Gupta · 26 December 2017

Situated 160 Kms north of Leh, Nubra Valley is a place rarely missed by any tourist visiting Leh-Ladakh. The fascinating ride on Double Hump Camels of Nubra in Ladakh is worth a visit. You would be not lucky, if, on the particular day, inclement weather restricts your entry into this fabled Valley. As it has happened many times in the past according the Jimmy, the Car driver. Luckily, on this day the weather looked clear and propitious, promising an enchanting drive to the valley.

Khardung-La

So driving 34 Kms from Leh, you pass through an awesome place called Khardung-La. Situated at an astounding height of 18380 Mts above sea level, Khardung-La is recorded as the highest motorable road in the World. You are overwhelmed with the sense of joie-di-vivre as Jimmy, carefully negotiates the car on the road that is surrounded on both side with silvery white snow.

Imagine a scene wherein a slight extension of your hand out of window, will enable you to touch and enjoy the titillating chill of this magnificence. All Smiles .. Right ?? Tourists, without exception, therefore ensure to halt here, at-least for some time, to carry with them some memorable moments.

Then moving towards Nubra Valley, Jimmy, cuts his ‘Innova’ through one mountain to another with amazing proficiency. After nearly four and a half hours of captivating drive you reach the outskirts of the Valley.

Nubra Valley

Ensconced between the Saltoro & Sasermuztag ranges, sub-divisions of the famous Karakoram Range, Nubra valley is hugely deprived of rains. The surrounding tall ranges inhibit the clouds to enter in here resulting in large stretches getting converted in to quintessential desert. Sand Dunes at Sumur & Hundar are resultant of this low rain effect.

“Double Hump Camels” also known as “Double Humped Bactrian Camels” are omnipresent in this expanse of desert. One can see these innocent herbivores obediently walk on the soft Sand along their respective masters.

Bumpy ride on these ceaselessly chewing desert animals, after they are colorfully saddled, is what all tourists look forward for. But it is not free. Price for this exciting experience: Rs 200/- for a 15 Minutes stroll & 350 for 30 minutes.

After you pay the Rs 350, you are allotted the camel. On reaching this Camel squatting on the sand, you are guided and then helped to climb and sit over it. The Camel then unfolds his legs and slowly gets up on his feet.

You on a 30 Minute ride that would take you deeper into the desert. As you endure this real bumpy nonetheless fascinating ride you keep looking around for something special. Incidentally, there is just sand all around except hills surrounding the area from all sides. After 30 minutes of ride you slowly get down from you Camel and walk back to your car.

You then go on to visit the nearby famous, “Diskit Monastery”.

Diskit Monastery

Diskit Monastery is facing the Shyok River which is a tributary of Indus River. This Monastery is deeply appreciated for it impressive architecture and its huge 32 meters tall Jampa Buddha Statue surmounted on the top of this revered place.

Apart from the statue of Cho Rinpoche (Crowned Buddha) in the prayer hall, there is a huge drum and several images of fierce guardian deities. An elevated cupola of the monastery depicts a fresco of the Tashilhunpo Monastery of Tibet.

After spending some time admiring its various facets you move further.

20 Kms towards Siachen, you come across a Lake called Lohan Lake. Surrounded by Hills, it is also called the “Secret Lake”, presumably because the existence of this lake gets revealed only after one reaches near its banks.

Little further is another sacrosanct place of the Buddhist worship – “Ensa Monastery”. Tired, you now check-in at the pre-booked resort “Lchang Nang Retreat” for a night halt.

Lchang Naang Retreat

Mr Stoben, the young owner of ‘Lchang Naang Retreat’, during his customary courtesy visit to guest cottages, enthusiastically extolled his property spread over 20 Acers.

During the course of our conversation, he revealed that the old ‘Silk Route’, established during the ‘Han Dynasty’ in China (operational from 2 BC to 14 AD) connecting China to the west, passed through Nubra Valley,and from just in front of this resort.

Adding he says : Nubra thus was considered to be the gateway to India during those times and the language spoken by then natives was called “Ladakhi Yarkandi”.

“My Grandfather still speaks this language” says Stoben with pride.

After a night halt at Nubra Valley, the next day journey was to the exceptionally picturesque “Pangong Lake” situated near the Indo-China border. This lake has become increasingly famous and popular after some scenes of Amir Khan starrer “3 Idiots” were shot here.

However, before going to sleep, there was a strict advisory from Jimmy, the driver, and corroborated by the Hotel Manager. “We should leave as early as possible next morning“. Reason: As the Sun rises, its heat starts melting the glaciers resulting in instant floods at times. To travel then becomes extremely dangerous. So, next Morning at dot 6, you take some packed breakfast and soon are on the way to Pangong.

Goodbye to Nice Nubra.

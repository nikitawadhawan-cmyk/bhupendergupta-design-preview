Sight Seeing Srinagar in unprecedented Strife

By Bhupender Gupta · 28 September 2017

18th June 2017, Pakistan defeated India in ICC Champions Trophy at Oval. You spend the night in cacophony of bursting Crackers totally dismayed, wondering why our youth in Kashmir had strayed. Were they betrayed or were they misguided???

Next morning Faroque with his Shikara is there sharp at 8 and takes you across the lake. Adil is waiting patiently there with his car ready to take you for Sight Seeing Srinagar.

So along with you camera you embark on this tour.

Hazratbal Dargah across Dal lake

Hazratbal Dargah in Srinagar, a hugely mesmerizing marble structure situated on the left banks of fascinating Dal Lake is your first halt. It is difficult to discern which one is more compelling. The lake to your left or the Dargah on your right.

However, after a few clicks you remove your shoes, cover your head and slowly walk through the walkway inside the Dargah. A middle-aged Maulvi inside, points at the floor above says, “there is kept the sacred hair of Prophet Mohammad (Moi-e-Muqqadus) as a revered relic since 1700 AD. The auspicious sight of this holy relic opens to public only on certain religious occasions.” So you pay your obeisance and quietly walk out.

Dal Lake

Next passing from outside the University of Srinagar on the Western side of Dal Lake, Adil informs that this prestigious University was established in 1948 and is spread over 263 acres of land. Remarkably. the 6 huge Gates located in six different corners of the University make the sight truly impressive.

Then as the car cruises around the legendary Dal Lake, Adil points towards three tiny islets right in-side the Lake. Two of them have incredible impressive Chinar trees grown over it. One of these islet is named “Char-Chinari” (four Chinars) as it has 4 Chinar trees grown on its four corners. You count. They are indeed four of them. Click, Click, Click….and they are captured, obviously in your camera.

Adil then steers his car towards the reputed Shalimar Garden or Shalimar Bagh.

Shalimar Bagh

Tragically, 10 Shops on the road leading towards Shalimar Bagh are burnt post Indo-Pak match last night. As s result, the security forces are compelled to block this way. Hence there is no option except to give up on this visit. Sad, but never mind.

Nishant Garden

Next you reach – ‘Nishat Garden’ also known as ‘The Heritage Mughal Garden Nishat’ or the ‘Garden of Joy’. Driving through famous Vakil Colony and then from in front of the Governor’s house, you reach this second largest garden of Kashmir.

Situated on the eastern side of Dal Lake with Zabarwan Mountains in its backdrop, the whole place looks stunningly beautiful. The garden’s awesome design rising from the edge of Dal Lake has murmuring water cascading down through its twelve artificially created terraces. Not to mention of the countless indescribably delightful flowery plants lined up that make every soul fill with inexpressible joy. Entry fees for this eternal Joy – just Rs. 20/- .

Tulip Garden

The another famous, Tulip Gardens is just 8-10 minutes away. Situated on the foothills of Zabarwan Range with an over view of the Dal Lake, it is spread over 30 hectares and is supposedly the largest Tulip Garden of Asia.

Regretfully it is deserted on the day. And why not ?? With not a single Tulip plant alive except for some dried stems, who will come. Full bloom of Tulip flowers occur only for around 15 days each year (mostly between 1st April and 15th April, during spring).

Garden opens for tourists only after 15th March as plants prepare for bloom. Hence for those wanting to enjoy and take selfie with the blooming tulips should schedule their visit accordingly. Entry fees here is Rs. 50/- for Adult and Rs. 25/- for kids.

Chashme Shahi

Close by is another interesting place known as Chashme Shahi (Chashma in urdu means ‘water spring’).

After paying Rs 20/- for an adult and extra 10/- if accompanied by a child, and you are allowed inside. Climb up a few dozen steps and you witness set of beautiful fountains placed at different levels.

Above is a simple but conspicuous structure where visitors queue up to taste pristine spring water flowing straight from within the hills. As you too spread your palm and taste this exceptionally sweet-cool water, you cannot help but smile & exclaim ‘Wha- kya baat hai’.

Incidentally, this sweet water is reputed to have medicinal qualities too.

This water then flows down and is effectively used to create amazing fountains on downhill terraces. Capturing these amazing sequences would be any photographers delight and you surely are not the one to let it go.

Pari Mahal

Time is the enemy. But you try to make up for the last destination of the day…. The Pari Mahal. This 17th Century constructed Pari Mahal situated above the Chashme Shahi Garden and towards the South-West of the Dal Lake was reportedly built over the ruins of an ancient Buddhist Monastery by Dara Shukoh, the eldest son of the 5th Mogul Emperor Shah Jahan. (recap : Dara Shukon being the eldest son of Shah Jahan was the heir-apparent to the throne. Sadly, his younger brother Aurangzeb captured and killed him in August 1659 and declared him as the Emperor. Rest is history).

Pari Mahal Garden’s high altitude makes it a vantage site to get a comprehensive and captivating view of the massive Dal Lake and its amazing surroundings. Just as you are riveted to the scenery around, rain gods decided to descend. Thereafter, the falling drops dramatically changed the hue, the color & the mood of the entire landscape. Thus standing below a stone structure your admiration for this heavenly splendor ratchets as your Camera goes on ceaselessly capturing the magnificence of Srinagar from here, there and everywhere.

Just while driving towards the Houseboat at Lake Nigel, you look out and enjoy the cool breeze hitting your face.

Immediately, the famous couplet of Emperor Jahangir describing Kashmir rings into your ears that said : “Gar firdaus, ruhe zamin ast, hamin asto, hamin asto, hamin asto”. (Meaning : If there is a heaven on earth, it is here, it is here, it is here.); How true…….

Emperor Jahangir

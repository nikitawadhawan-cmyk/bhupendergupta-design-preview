To Rotorua from Waitomo Caves, Land of mesmerizing Geysers

By Bhupender Gupta · 4 September 2017

Drive 145 km south of Auckland to reach Rotorua from Waitomo Caves, land of mesmerizing geysers and Maori warmth. Rotorua incidentally is also known as ‘Thermal City’.

So, passing via Arapuni Road and NH5, within the northern Island of New Zealand (NZ is divided in to two Islands, the North & the South), you enter into air filled with distinct Sulphur aroma. An unmistakable sign of the arrival of the city – ‘Rotorua’.

Defined as the heartland of the warm & welcoming Maori culture, its utter raw beauty is expressed by its fascinating spouting geysers, active volcanoes, bubbling mud pools, sparkling lakes & large swathes of alluring lush forests. The one place that encompasses nearly all of these amazing characteristics is – “Te Puia”.

“Te Puia” spans within 70 hectares of historic ‘Te Whakarewarewa Geothermal Valley’. With prior booking priced at 117 NZ Dollars per adult & 58.50 for a child, you move around in small groups accompanied by an erudite Maori guide.

Guy, the group guide for this evening, is a amiable and amusingly humorous man. A little over 30, Guy tirelessly extols his tribe’s riveting past with remarkable pride. He takes you back in time when Maoris, originally from Polynesia, accidentally land on the alluring shores of NZ.

These lot of Maoris, presumably got so impressed by the beauty of this place, that was full of flora, fauna and abundant fish, that they decided to settle here for good. Sadly the occasional but horrifying bloody strife that happened among them, primarily over the land domains, sometimes saw an entire warring group wiped out in a single night.

After entering this place, Kiwi house, a dark place with minimal light, is the first place where Guy takes you in. Here an unexpectedly shy, flightless New Zealand’s endangered National bird – the shaggy feathered – ‘Kiwi’ becomes the cynosure of all eyes. No flash please, you are forewarned. You obey and slowly & silently walk towards the exit.

As you proceed a few meters ahead, you are suddenly hit by the warm & misty air. Reason: Swathes of amazing mud pools bubbling with boiling water have added significant degrees to the atmospheric temperature. Guy informs you that the locals here use this hot steam ingeniously. It acts as a natural steam vent cooker that helps cook food and preserve eatables.

Now coming tantalizingly close to a spectacularly active ‘Pohutu’ Geyser (‘Pohutu’ means ‘constant splashing’ in local Maori language) you stand spellbound and motionless. In complete awe, your eyes remain fixated to this incredible wonder.

‘Pohutu’ sprouts every 45 minutes (caused due to release of intense pressure created as underground water comes in contact with the underlying Sulphur and gets over-heated subsequently turning into steam) and reaches an amazing height of over 100 feet into the air. “Wow” is an exclamation that instantly reverberates as dazed spectators stand engulfed in the flowing mist.

The splashed Sulphur-rich frothy water then slowly cascades down the rocky surface depositing parts of Sulphur on its way. It later flows into the rivulet that then merges into the adjoining lake.

Your deep reluctance to leave the place long after it’s all over speaks volumes of the impression this nature’s marvel has inadvertently created over you.

Speechless as you are, Guy, not alien to such reactions, smiles and prods you go towards ‘Powhiri’.

Powhiri – is a traditional Maori welcome ceremony that is performed by the aborigines (Original inhabitants of NZ). Here the Maoris demonstrate a unique & amusing way how they greet their guests.

So, dressed in attractive and colorful hand woven tribal dresses with necklaces and pendants of stones & bones, these aborigines (Males) barge towards you with their long tongues lashing out, eyes wide and red as if to display their power through intimidation. You do feel wary albeit momentarily.

However, soon their lashing tongues move in, their eyes contract, and smiles on their faces flash wide. You begin to feel being showered with warmth and compassion, typical of Maori style of welcome.

Immediately thereafter, the traditionally dressed beautiful Maori women, with their disarming smile, escort you towards a hall and the bonhomie between you and them is complete.

Once inside, you are now entertained with the series of well-coordinated & immaculately choreographed tribal dances.

After this enthralling experience the finale is at ‘Te Po’. Here an elaborate spread of traditional & indigenous Maori feast prepared in a typical Hangi style awaits you.

Ten Round tables with nine chairs around each complete the dinning set up. Hungry as you are, smiling Maoris watch you affectionately as you savor or even gluttonize their meticulously prepared dishes. As you bid good bye to your fabulous hosts, the exotic taste of mouthwatering culinary continues to linger even as you steer your car towards the next destination – The Capital City of New Zealand – ‘Willington’.
